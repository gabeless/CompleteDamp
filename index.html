Damp problems

Three kinds of damp, three different fixes.

They look alike on a wall but have different causes. This is why the survey comes first.

Rising damp

What you see

A tide mark and white salt deposits on lower walls, generally below about 1 m. Peeling paint and crumbling plaster.

Usual cause

Ground moisture drawn up through masonry, often where the damp-proof course has failed or is missing.

Usual remedy

New damp-proof course, then replastering with salt-resistant materials.

Penetrating damp

What you see

Damp patches that grow after rain, on external walls, chimney breasts, or ceilings below the roof.

Usual cause

Water entering through a defect such as blocked gutters, failed pointing, cracked render or roof faults.

Usual remedy

Repair the defect, let the wall dry, then make good the finishes.

Condensation

What you see

Black mould in corners and on ceilings, wet windows, and a musty smell, worse in cold weather.

Usual cause

Warm, humid air meeting cold surfaces, usually with poor ventilation.

Usual remedy

Better ventilation, heating and moisture control, plus mould treatment.
